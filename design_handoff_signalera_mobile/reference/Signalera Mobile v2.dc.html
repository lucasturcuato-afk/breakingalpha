The brief and the record are the same timeline.

v1 gave the record its own tab. That was the wrong instinct held confidently. It is what the app already is.

why this and not v1

Four arguments, and the three I expect to lose

1 · One timeline, because a claim is one object

v1 put the brief in Today and the record in Record, which meant the same claim appeared in two places under two designs and the user had to learn that they were the same thing. On the Ledger a claim enters at the top of today, gains your note when you commit, gains its attribution figures when the window closes, and settles into the past. One object, one anatomy, accreting evidence. The record is not somewhere you go. It is the app, older.

The structural consequence is that open calls sit above today, because their dates have not arrived. Scrolling down is your past reasoning; the thing you owe an answer on is the first thing on the screen. That is the return trigger made spatial rather than announced, and it is the Zeigarnik effect doing the work a notification cannot do on mobile web.

2 · The note is the product, so committing asks for it

This is the largest change and I believe it is the correct one. A record of adopted calls proves you clicked. A record of adopted calls each carrying one line of your own reasoning, timestamped before the outcome was known, proves you thought. Only the second is evidence, and evidence is the entire product thesis. So the commit sheet leads with the question rather than the button, and the button is not the first thing your thumb finds.

It also makes the friction honest. v1 asked for 700ms of pressure, which is theatre if nothing is being weighed. Here the pressure sits after a sentence you had to compose, so the gesture confirms a decision instead of manufacturing one.

3 · Resolution gets the whole screen, and reads you back to yourself

Screen 02 is the emotional centre and it is the only full-screen interruption in the design. Your claim, the figures, and then your own words from three weeks ago, before you knew. That confrontation is the product working. It is also why challenged entries cannot be punished visually: the note is what is being examined, and a note can be excellent while the window runs against it. Screen 02 says exactly that in the closing line.

4 · The prepared record, which nothing before this touched

The user cannot get an analyst seat without evidence they can think. Every version so far built the evidence and then left them to screenshot it. Screen 12 produces the artifact: complete, timestamped, uncurated, with the counts stated plainly and no rate derived from them. The line that does the work is the one under the name, saying every entry is included and nothing is sorted or removed. A record you can filter before showing it is not evidence, and this is the screen where that principle either survives or gets quietly sold.

Where I expect to lose

Typing on a commute
The required note is the strongest idea here and the most likely to be cut. One-handed typing on a moving train is genuinely hard, and if adoption drops the loop starves. My answer is that a call you would not spend twenty seconds justifying was not a call. If you disagree, the fallback is a note that can be added later with the entry marked as unjustified until it is, which preserves the principle and loses the friction.
Counts on screen 12
Four numbers side by side invite the reader to divide them. My position is that a complete count is a fact and a rate is a claim, and that hiding the counts would make the record less auditable rather than more compliant. But this is the single element most likely to fail legal review, and the design survives their removal without a redraw.
Two words are gone
Radar and Dashboard both disappear, and Track this call becomes Take this call, because you are taking a position of reasoning rather than subscribing to a feed. Renames are cheap to reverse and I would not fight hard for any of the three.
Deal Flow, again
Same loss as before and I have not solved it. Deals live inside Watch as a filter, so there is no surface for surveying the whole deal universe. For a private equity recruiting audience that may be a real job, and if it is, Watch needs a fourth filter state that shows everything rather than only what you follow.
Two departures from the design system, isolated and flagged

The system puts a 3px gold spine on the left of scored objects and a 4px gold rule on the lead card. Your ban on coloured left borders overrides both, so state lives in a 2px top edge plus a dot and the state word, applied consistently everywhere. Second, gold never touches text at any size anywhere in this design: it survives as fills, rules, dots and icons only, which is stricter than the system's own link colour and comes out of measuring the v1 screens rather than trusting them.
