Two craft defects found by reading, not by looking

Both of these had passed every audit I had run, because I had been measuring whether things were legible and reachable rather than whether they were well set.

The seven connective surfaces, and why precedent picked them

I went looking for the right precedent set before adding anything. The temptation with a finance product is to benchmark against terminals, and that is the wrong shelf: a terminal assumes a seated professional with two monitors and no time pressure on any single glance. The shelf this belongs on is the publication reader, which is also exactly where the design system points, since it asks for a newspaper you would pay for rather than a dashboard you would abandon. Read against that set, the structure here stands up: a finite daily edition, reader-view typography, saving for offline, and following topics rather than assembling widgets.

What the comparison did expose is that we were missing the connective tissue every one of those apps has and no amount of good editorial design substitutes for. Seven surfaces, all now built, all reachable.

Search was the worst omission. Ask had a field that did nothing, which is the same defect I have been fixing all week: a control asserting a capability it does not have. It now types, and results are grouped by what the thing is, with your own ledger entries ranked among companies and deals rather than hidden behind a tab. The no-result state says what coverage actually runs to instead of apologising.

Story is the reader view, and it earns its place by ending where no general reader can: the piece closes by showing the open call it bears on, so reading is continuous with the record rather than adjacent to it. Deal detail gives every Deal Flow row somewhere to go, with the process as a dated timeline, because for this audience the sequence is the story. Saved is the offline case: the brief is kept automatically for the current day, since the commute is exactly where signal fails.

Settings is where theme now properly lives, and it doubles as the map of everything not on a tab. Alerts is the one I would defend hardest: it is deliberately short, and it says out loud that nothing here can interrupt you in a browser tab and that the product does not depend on it. A settings page that promised notifications it cannot deliver would undo the whole return-trigger argument. Sign in is the last piece needed to hand this to an engineer.

One compliance catch worth recording. The sign-in screen carried the positioning line verbatim from the brief, and that line contains a banned substring inside an ordinary word. It now reads that we track which calls the evidence supports, which is the approved vocabulary and arguably the truer claim. Worth raising with counsel, because the same line is on the marketing site.

Still unresolved

Typing a note on a commute is still the biggest risk in the design and building it did not make it smaller. Intelligence is the weakest of the three Ask modes, because a pattern reading of your own record is the hardest thing in the product to make trustworthy and the easiest to make glib. Transcripts and Comps stay unbuilt, because the repo itself has not built them.

One honest deviation to flag rather than bury: the inline citation anchors are a 17px glyph. Nothing set in flowing prose can be a 44px target without wrecking the line, so they carry an invisible expanded hit box that measures 47px, and every source additionally has a 59px row in the list at the foot of the memo. No source is reachable only through the small anchor.

The anchors sit after the sentence period rather than before it, which is the publishing convention and also the only way to make the typography work here. A padded, bordered pill cannot sit flush against following punctuation: its padding and border push the period six pixels clear of the bracket, which is further than the word space after it, so the period reads as orphaned. Placing the anchor after the stop binds the period to the word it terminates and leaves exactly one word space before the next sentence.
